**Titles revised 16 September 2026, second pass.** The first draft used positioning labels as
titles rather than queries people actually type. Every title below is now the search phrasing,
verified against live SERPs where noted. The angle lives in the thesis, not the headline.

**Titling convention, added 16 September 2026.** Studied bycrawford.com/blog, which ranks hard
in an adjacent niche. Every title there is **two parts: the query, then a payload**, joined by a
colon, a question mark or a parenthetical. The first half catches the search. The second half
is the reason to click this result rather than the nine identical ones around it. A bare query
ranks and loses the click.

Apply it to every title in this queue and every future one:

- `[the query people type]` + `:` or `?` + `[the specific promise, number, contrast or finding]`
- Second person. "your website", not "my website". People search "my", publishers write "your".
- A number in the payload where the article genuinely has one.
- Year only where the answer expires, and only in the SEO title.

**Do not copy two things from Crawford.** Their Title Case violates CLAUDE.md section 14, which
requires sentence case everywhere including the H1 and SEO title. And their listicle density
suits a volume play; this blog wins on depth and a measured claim, not on "7 best" roundups.

**Standing note on crowded queries.** Three of these four sit in well-served genres. That is
accepted deliberately: in this niche the uncrowded queries have almost no demand, and each of
these is crowded with generic advice against which a measured result or a real pattern wins.
Where the gap score is 1 or 2, the ownable claim is doing the work and the post is not worth
writing without it.

### 2. Will a website redesign hurt your Google rankings? What happened to one site over 90 days

- **Slug:** `website-redesign-google-rankings`

- **Primary question:** "will a website redesign hurt my rankings" - verified 16 Sept 2026,
  a settled genre with consistent phrasing across page one
- **Source:** Bel'istria. Impressions up 99% over 90 days, measured in Google Search Console,
  captured 26 August 2026. Already published with that caption, so the claim is on the record.
- **Thesis:** Most redesign advice is about avoiding damage. The interesting question is what
  actually moved, and the answer is rarely the visual design.
- **Category:** SEO & AEO -> links up to `/services/seo-aeo-ppc`, proof link to the case study
- **Score 9/12** - Demand 3 - Gap 1 - Commercial fit 3 - Ownable 3
  - **Gap revised down to 1 after checking the SERP.** This is a crowded genre. What is absent
    is not advice, it is evidence: every page one result is a checklist for avoiding damage
    and not one publishes a measured outcome. Win on the number, not on the explanation.
  - Ownable 3 is the whole post. Competitors publish advice; this publishes a measured result
    with a stated window and a named source. That is the "number only we have" the article
    skill requires, and it is the strongest asset on the site.
- **Cannibalisation check: NARROW PASS, watch it.** This query sits next to the guides. The
  guides own how to preserve rankings. This owns what happened to one site and why. If a
  draft starts explaining redirect mapping, it has drifted and the section gets cut.
- **Ownable claim:** the 99% figure, with what was counted and over what period, plus what
  specifically changed on the site. Client named, since the case study already exists.
- **Must not claim:** this is one site over one window. Say so plainly. No generalisation to
  "redesigns increase impressions", no percentage applied to any other project, and nothing
  about rankings or revenue that was not measured.
- **Screenshots:** the Search Console impressions chart, same capture as the published caption.
- **Publish:** 6 October 2026
- **LinkedIn derivative:** one number, what was counted, and the part of the rebuild that
