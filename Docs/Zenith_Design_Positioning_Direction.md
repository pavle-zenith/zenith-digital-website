## 4. The shared aesthetic — and Zenith's version

Every one of these sites speaks the same visual language, and it's exactly the "meaningful refresh" lane you picked:

- **Premium minimalism.** High-contrast neutral palettes — dark charcoal/black (DesignMe, Squareblack), deep navy (Flow Ninja), or clean white + navy + a warm accent (by Crawford). No decorative noise, no gradients-for-gradients'-sake.
- **Type is the hero.** Large, bold, modern sans-serif headlines carrying the page; generous whitespace; short, punchy copy blocks — never dense paragraphs.
- **Motion shows the work, not itself.** Autoplay case-study video, smooth scroll reveals, logo/case carousels. Animation demonstrates craft rather than decorating.
- **Real work over stock.** Bespoke project screenshots, client logos in consistent monochrome, human founder/team photography.
- **Specificity everywhere.** Metrics, named clients, award badges, review counts — credibility through numbers, not adjectives.

**Zenith's take:** keep your brand DNA but push into this premium-minimal, type-led, dark-or-high-contrast direction with motion that shows real client work. One differentiator to consider: since three references are neutral/monochrome, a single confident **signature accent color** (owned consistently) would help you stand out *and* fight the "which Zenith?" brand-collision problem from the GSC data. (I'll pull your exact current palette during scaffolding so we refine from your real colors, not guesses.)
